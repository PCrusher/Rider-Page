Gravel Nationals: what went wrong at kilometre 90

12 Sep 20266 min readRace report
cover.jpg · 16:9

The first 90 kilometres were the best racing I have done all year. The lead group stayed together over the gravel climbs, nobody wasted energy, and I felt almost lazy on the wheel.

Then the road turned into a headwind section along the lake, and my right calf locked up. I had skipped the feed zone at 80 kilometres because I felt fine, which is exactly the thing I tell other people not to do.

The decision on the last climb

With 25 kilometres left I had two options: sit in and hope for a sprint, or go early and take the risk. The route is on Strava if you want to look at the profile.

I would rather lose trying something than lose waiting for it.

Here is the onboard footage from the final 10 kilometres. You can see the exact moment I look back and decide not to wait.

YouTube embed

youtube.com/watch?v=dQw4w9WgXcQ

I finished third, 14 seconds behind the winner. Next year the feed zone at 80 kilometres is not optional.
